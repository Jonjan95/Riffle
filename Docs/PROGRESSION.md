# Alder Creek progression foundation

## Scope

The chapter extends the approved URP state (b929e4d) on **progression-foundation**. It retains the existing manual Work / Wash / E Collect loop, pan/input architecture, scene, and camera. Only Alder Creek is included.

## Economy and order

| Purchase | Costs by level | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Better pan | 8 / 60 / 140 | Existing +18% processing efficiency per level; brass inlays |
| Better riffles | 10 / 50 / 120 | Existing +35% capture accumulation per level; modest raised accents |
| Richer scoop | 12 / 90 / 200 | +3 gold per load per level; another tool on the rack |
| Auto Work | 80 | All tools at level 1 |
| Auto Wash | 220 | Auto Work and all tools at level 2 |
| Auto Collect | 190 | Auto Wash and all tools at level 3 |

All twelve purchases total 1,180 gold. There is one currency. Tools can be purchased in any order; the next-goal card recommends the least expensive missing tool in the current assistance tier. Helpers remain gated so money alone cannot skip the chapter.

## Actions and automation

Auto Work supplies `PanIntent.Work`. With no Auto Wash, it prepares material and then pauses once compacted sediment is nearly gone, capture is established, and stones have cleared. The player supplies Wash and Collect.

Auto Wash supplies `PanIntent.Wash`. Alone, it washes available loose material and pauses at `NeedsWork`. Manual Work can prepare the remaining material.

With both on, `PanAutomation` alternates:
- Work until at least 42% loose material is available with stratification at least 55%, or compacted dirt is nearly exhausted (4.5%).
- Wash until `NeedsWork` reports depleted loose material and remaining compacted dirt.
- Repeat, then finish washing the concentrate.

An 0.8-second minimum phase prevents chattering; this is not a player timing window. Tests verify that both intents are never simultaneously generated by combined assistance. Manual input takes priority for that frame, including an explicitly held combination, and releasing resumes assistance from the material state.

Auto Collect only requests the existing `CreekGame.Collect()` when the simulation is ready, after a 2.2-second reveal. The same pay-once collection path credits the gold and prepares a new scoop. Assistance pauses with Pause, Help, and loss of focus. Ownership and ON/OFF state persist independently.

## Visual chapter

- First collection adds a cedar supply crate with folded marigold cloth.
- Scoop levels add enamel scoops to a tool rack beside the tent.
- Pan levels add brass inlays; riffles gain a small visible rise.
- At 40 lifetime gold the existing wheel and sluice flow activate, with a small screen basket.
- Auto Work adds a teal housing, slow brass crank and pan linkage.
- Auto Wash adds a small water header with an animated stream during Wash.
- Auto Collect adds a catch table, brass tray and a few visible findings.

These are small additions around the saved camp. The pan remains dominant. Reset hides all added milestones; save restoration reactivates the appropriate ones.

The sluice was already a visual milestone, not a passive payout producer. It now affects throughput: a fresh load begins with 72% compacted / 28% loose material instead of 82% / 18%. Total sediment and the load's gold value remain unchanged. Work and Wash are still both needed.

## Gold and feedback

Existing gold clustering and quiet readiness/collection audio are retained. A persistent small charcoal pocket makes the finish readable; tiny leftover silt visuals disappear at readiness, while leftover stones remain visible. A restrained four-point glint and a gold-gain badge beside the balance reinforce collection without covering the pan.

Every sixth load includes one larger flake (+1 gold), and every twentieth includes a small nugget (+2 instead). The completed-pan count seeds each load, so restart cannot reroll its contents. There is no inventory, rarity currency or item collection system.

## Saving

`SaveStore` writes a small versioned text record:
gold, lifetime earnings, pan/riffle/scoop levels, owned helper bits, enabled helper bits, completed pans.

Milestones derive from those durable values. No particle state, current pan transform, timer, or offline elapsed time is saved. Normal Windows saves live under Unity's persistent data folder in `alder-creek-v1.txt`. Verification modes redirect to unique directories in `Playtest/Verification`.

Writes go through a temporary file and atomic replacement, keeping the previous save as `.bak`. A valid backup recovers a corrupt primary; unreadable files are preserved with a visible warning instead of being overwritten by a fresh game. Reset is a two-step Pause action and removes all three save files.

## Pacing evidence

The first cost pass simulated at 35–47 minutes with an eight-minute purchase gap. Prices were reduced before delivery.

Final deterministic route (three-second Work / five-second Wash holds until assistance, cheapest recommended tool first):

| Milestone | Focused route | Unhurried route |
|---|---:|---:|
| Three first-level tools | 1.9 min | 3.2 min |
| Auto Work | 4.6 min | 6.5 min |
| All tools level 2 | 8.4 min | 11.9 min |
| Auto Wash | 11.5 min | 16.1 min |
| All tools level 3 | 18.4 min | 25.5 min |
| Auto Collect | 20.7 min | 28.7 min |

Both simulated routes use 79 completed pans. The Windows full-game path uses about 80. Estimates include a brief initial learning allowance, 1.5–4 seconds at gold readiness, 1–3 seconds between scoops, and 5–12 seconds reading each purchase. These allowances model the player; they are not imposed in game. Longest purchase gap is 3.1–4.2 minutes, after Auto Work. Manual simultaneous holds and optimized purchases can be faster.

## URP polish

The custom matte shader now allows per-renderer shadow attenuation. Pan surfaces receive only 18% of the normal shadow contrast, reducing the distracting wedges inside the bowl. Thin sediment beds and small grains do not cast shadows. The cel lighting, palette, orthographic camera and surrounding creek lighting remain.

The original high-level riffle scale lifted the entire arcs away from the bowl. Its visual rise is reduced to 3% per level; the original simulation benefit is unchanged.

Broad environmental shadows and the simple flat water remain candidates for a later polish pass. Taller windows naturally crop more peripheral scenery; the pan and progression UI remain usable. There is no claim of a frame-time benchmark.
